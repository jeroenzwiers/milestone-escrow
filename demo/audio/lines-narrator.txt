Sanne runs a bakery. She wants online lunch orders by next week, and she has three hundred euros.

Nobody sells that as one package. She'd need a developer, someone for the menu, and a courier, and she'd have to coordinate them and pay up front.

With Milestone Escrow she only states the goal and the budget. Agents do the rest.

A planner agent asks providers for offers. Each provider is an agent with its own private terms.

The agency declines: it's booked for two weeks.

The cheap courier only rides on days with ten or more orders, which is risky in a launch week. The planner weighs that, picks the courier without a minimum, and submits a plan.

Fixed rules check it: within budget, and every price exactly what the provider agreed to.

Sanne approves and pays once, with PayPal.

The money sits in escrow. Nobody is paid until their part is verified.

Then the courier drops out.

The planner replans. It asks the other courier to drop its minimum, gets a no, and adjusts the plan around it.

Sanne sees exactly what changes, what comes back to her and what is new to pay. Nothing moves until she approves.

Each provider delivers.

The reviewer opens Daan's pull request and checks it against every criterion. The confirmation email is missing, so his money stays in escrow, and he gets concrete feedback.

The courier's delivery log checks out, criterion by criterion.

After a short window in which Sanne could object, PayPal pays the courier directly.

The agents negotiate and judge, but they never move money on their own. Code checks every plan, decides when a payout is allowed, and every change needs Sanne's approval.

Everything the agents did is on the record.

Give it a goal and a budget, and the team that can make it happen comes together, and gets paid when the work is done.
